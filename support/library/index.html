Recipes

A look you like can be saved as a recipe - the whole treatment, grain and tone and all, where a look alone is only the colour - and applied in one tap, on the camera or afterwards - and sent to a friend as a file, with the film look travelling alongside it. The library's Create button holds the composers: carousels, photos laid across a strip of Instagram slides with one picture spanning the cut between two, and contact sheets - your frames on 35mm film, a lab strip or proof paper, drawn down to the sprockets and the edge printing.

Edit a whole roll Darkroom

Give many photos the same treatment at once - an edit from one photo, or any recipe.

  1. To use an edit you have made: open that photo in the editor, tap More, then Copy Edit. (Skip this to use a recipe instead.)
  2. In the library, tap Select and choose the photos.
  3. Tap Apply, then Paste Edit or a recipe, and confirm.

Each photo keeps its own crop, straightening and retouching - only the look and the adjustments are applied, exactly as a recipe is. Every photo can still be changed on its own afterwards, and nothing is baked in. Videos in a selection are left as they are.

The community

The Community page in Recipes is a shelf of looks Haku users have shared - hold any one to see it rendered large on three photographs, tap to open it, add it in a tap. Vote for the ones you like, and offer your own from the recipe's own menu. Most are built on the looks Haku ships with, so they render exactly as their author saw them with nothing to download; one built on an imported look tells you which file it needs and where to find it.

Browse them on the web at hakufilmcamera.app/recipes. Every recipe there has a Report this recipe link if something about it is wrong.

Video

The PHOTO/VIDEO switch under the viewfinder turns the shutter into a record button - or just hold it in photo mode. The take itself is recorded clean and the look sits over it as a recipe, so playback is graded live and the film simulation is still yours to change afterwards.

Nothing is cut from the recording itself; drag the ends back out to have it all again.

Frame

The shape of the picture: aspect and quarter-turns.

A recording keeps the shape it was shot at; this re-frames it for where it is going - square for a grid, 4:3 to match your photographs.

Cropping a 16:9 take to 1:1 throws away the sides. The original recording is untouched, so it can always be re-framed.

Fujifilm's looks

They publish them free, and Film Looks now downloads the right archive directly - no computer, no hunting for the correct file. This is the one thing worth doing before you go and shoot: it is the difference between 10 looks and 20. And if you follow the recipe sites, paste any published Fujifilm recipe into Recipes - Haku reads the settings straight off the text, or off a screenshot.

Importing looks is part of the Darkroom. Haku is not associated with the Fujifilm Corporation; the files are theirs, published free.
